# The clipper (7 November 2026, branch `clipper`, merged; the iPhone side 8 November)

Part of [the plan](../PLAN.md).

**Why:** articles worth keeping are read in the browser, and copying one into a note by hand (its text, title, author, address) is work enough that it doesn't happen. A clip should be one click, and give a note as well formed as one written by hand, of the right kind.

**Decided:** Point does the clipping, not the browser. The extension only sends the page's address, its title and any selected text, as `point://clip?url=…&title=…&selection=…`. Point fetches the page itself and finds its article in a web view no one sees, where the page is only parsed: its own scripts never run and nothing it refers to is loaded. That keeps the extension tiny (and the same for any browser, or the iPhone's share sheet later), and the clipping tested in Swift. The cost: Point fetches without the browser's sign-ins, so a page behind a login or a paywall may give a note with only its link.

**Built:**
- **ClipKit** (a new package): `ClipExtractor` fetches the page and runs Mozilla's Readability (finds the article) and Turndown with its GFM plugin (writes it as Markdown) in a hidden `WKWebView`, both bundled with their licences (`THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.txt`). `clip.js` adds what pages say about themselves: authors and the publication date from structured data and meta tags, the site's name. Swift then tidies it: the site's name off the end of the title, tracking parameters (`utm_…`, `fbclid`) off the address, bylines into names, and the Markdown as Point writes it (`- ` and `1. ` lists, no soft hyphens, stray indents, trailing spaces or runs of empty lines).
- **The note** (`ClipNote`): the clip kind's first template, filled in: `title`, `author` (as links to people), `published` (if known), `format` (Article, or Video for YouTube and Vimeo), `source` and `saved`, with `type` set to the kind unless the template sets one. Selected text comes first, as a quote, then the template's own text, then the article.
- **The Mac:** opens `point://clip` links (without opening a window). The clip kind is the one named Media (or Clipping, Clip, Web clip, Article); its folder and template are used, else a `Clippings` folder. The new note opens in a new tab, named as its title (without the day, since 11 November). A page clipped before (the same `source`) opens its note instead. A notice says what's happening: "Clipping “…”…", then "Clipped to Clippings", "Clipped before", or "Couldn't read the page, so the note has its link". The app can now reach the network (`com.apple.security.network.client`), to fetch pages.
- **Shared by both apps** (`Clipping`, in ClipKit): which kind clips are, reading the page or settling for its link, how a page clipped before is recognised, and the note's title and text. Each app makes the note and shows how it went in its own way. ClipKit's scripts are in `Scripts`, not `Resources`: an iOS bundle can't have a top-level folder of that name (signing fails).
- **The iPhone:** opens `point://clip` links the same way, with a capsule above the search bar saying how it's going, then opens the new note. A share-sheet Shortcut sends them (`Clipper/iPhone`, which has the steps). Pressing and holding **+** offers **Clip copied link** too, for a link copied anywhere.
- **The extension** (`Clipper/Chrome`, for Dia, Chrome and other Chromium browsers; loaded unpacked, see its README): its button or ⌥⇧P clips the page; selected text is kept as a quote; right-clicking a link clips the page it goes to.

**Tried:** `ClipTests` (filling the Media template, no template, a template that sets its own type, clip addresses, bylines, dates and addresses, tidying Markdown, extracting an article with nested and numbered lists). A live clip of a real blog post (`POINT_CLIP_URL=… swift test --filter LiveClipTests` in `Packages/ClipKit`).

On the iPhone, in the Simulator: a `point://clip` link, with Point open and with it closed, made a Media-less vault's note in `Clippings` with its title, author, published, format, source and saved; the same page again, with `utm_` parameters added, opened that note ("Clipped before").

**Not tried:** the Mac app end to end, from the browser (it builds; running a second copy beside Mike's would share its settings). Dia itself: whether it asks before opening Point, and whether the shortcut is free. The Shortcut itself (the Simulator has no Shortcuts app) and a real iPhone. **Clip copied link** with a link on the clipboard: the Simulator can't show iOS's "Allow paste" prompt and refuses the paste, so it only says "No link copied"; on an iPhone the prompt should appear.

**Fixed (11 November):**
- **Pages you're signed in to.** A Substack post for subscribers was clipped as the public preview ("Subscribe for free to continue reading"): Point fetched it without the browser's sign-ins. Now the extension sends the page as the browser shows it, its scripts, styles and drawn icons left out, compressed (raw deflate, base64url) in the link as `page=…`; Point reads that, and fetches the page only when none is sent (a link right-clicked, a page over the browser's 2 MB link limit) or no article is found in it. That post is an 11 KB link; macOS delivered a 1.9 MB one intact. The iPhone's Shortcut still sends only the address.
- **Missing headings.** Readability drops anything whose class or id sounds like the page around the article ("header", "comment", "share"…), and Substack's headings are `header-anchor-post`, so they vanished. Headings now lose their classes and ids, and the empty anchor buttons and icons inside them, before Readability runs; a heading that's all bold isn't also `**…**`, and one starting with a number isn't escaped (`#### 1. Are you…`, not `1\.`).
- **Linked pictures** are one line: the picture alone if the link is only to a bigger copy of it, else `[![…](…)](…)` (Turndown spread them over five).
- **File names:** a clip is named as its title alone (`How many jobs are you taking on.md`), not with the day; its title is its file name, as for other notes not named by Point.

- **Cruft around a newspaper article** (sent signed in, so the page as shown, with its newsletters, related and trending stories, ads and a gift offer docked at the bottom; its title, standfirst and lead caption twice; "Image" and "Credit..." for screen readers; a `source` of newsletter tracking, a `user_id` among it). The extension now leaves out what isn't shown, what's only for screen readers (one pixel square) and what's fixed over the page, and keeps the canonical link. Before Readability, `clip.js` keeps only the page's `<article>` when one holds most of the text, and drops what the page labels hidden, ads' labels, links styled as buttons, and anything whose test id, accessible label or component name says ad, promo, newsletter, related, trending, subscribe, share or the like (never what holds the headline or much of the text); captions and credits are kept apart. Tried on the real page, saved from the browser: its screen-reader labels had their own class (`…_accessibilityLabel`), now matched too, and the gift offer was marked `data-campaign` and `data-audience`, now dropped. Two pictures side by side in a box of their own went (Readability takes a box of pictures with little text for clutter), so such a box becomes one figure first. A date on a line of its own goes too (the note has `published`). Swift drops a heading that's the title, and a paragraph seen already, and takes `source` from the canonical link on the same site, else without more tracking parameters (`nl`, `user_id`, `emc` and the like).

- **Published dates** (later on 11 November): `published` is the year alone (`2026`), as in the vault's notes. With no date in the page's structured data or meta tags, the clipper took the first `<time>` anywhere on the page, which on a page clipped signed in was a course starting the next week. Now only a date by the headline counts (in a header holding it, or within three levels of it), or one marked `datePublished`; and a date after today is no date.

**Tried (11 November):** `ClipTests` (a page sent in the link, including one packed by a browser's `CompressionStream`; Substack-style headings; linked pictures). The live Substack post, fetched: all five headings. The extension's page-reading step run on that post in a browser: 146 KB of HTML, 43 KB stripped, an 11 KB link. Both apps build. The newspaper article, saved from the browser while signed in: through Point alone, and through the extension's own reading and packing run on it in a browser (a 30 KB link, read in 11 ms): the whole article, all its pictures and captions, nothing around it. `LiveClipTests` can take a saved page (`POINT_CLIP_FILE`) or a saved `point://clip` address (`POINT_CLIP_ADDRESS`). **Not tried:** the extension itself in Dia, end to end.
